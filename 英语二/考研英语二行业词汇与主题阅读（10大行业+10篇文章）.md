# 考研英语二行业词汇与主题阅读（10大行业+300词+10篇文章）

说明：这份资料按 10 个常见行业整理。每个行业包含两部分：

- 常用核心词表（音标 + 英汉对照）
- 1 篇对应行业主题文章

补充说明：音标以常见国际音标写法为主，个别单词在英式和美式发音中会有细微差异；词组按整体标注，方便你直接朗读和记忆。扩充后每个行业约 30 个高频词，更适合做行业主题积累和写作素材储备。

使用建议：

1. 先背本行业词表，重点记 "英文形式 + 音标 + 中文含义"；
2. 再读对应文章，注意文章中加粗的目标词；
3. 读完后尝试复述文章大意，并用词表里的单词自己造句；
4. 第二天回顾时，先遮住中文义再回忆，再遮住英文词做英汉互译。

### 文章 1：Improving a Digital Platform

At a growing tech company, a young **engineer** was asked to improve the **interface** of the firm's **digital** service **platform**. The old **software** often froze because the **database** had become too large and the **network** could not handle heavy traffic. When too many users tried to **access** the system on their **device** at the same time, the response became slow and unreliable. To solve the problem, the team designed a new **algorithm** that could **automate** several routine tasks and reduce pressure on the server. They also released a small **update** every week instead of waiting months for a major change. Just as important, they strengthened **security** and improved the protection of customer **privacy**, because trust matters as much as speed. Although new **hardware** was needed for testing, the company accepted the extra cost. In the end, the platform became faster, safer, and easier to use, showing that practical innovation often comes from steady improvement rather than dramatic invention.

### 文章 2：Why Profit Is Not Everything

A family business once believed that rising **revenue** meant it was in excellent condition, but the owner later discovered that the real situation was more complicated. Several banks offered a **loan**, yet taking on more **debt** would increase **interest** payments and reduce future **profit**. To make a realistic **forecast**, the owner examined every **transaction**, compared monthly **expense** reports, and created a stricter **budget**. He also reviewed the company's **asset** structure and studied the current **market** before making any new **investment**. The numbers revealed that weak **cash flow** was a greater problem than low sales. Money was entering the business too slowly, while bills had to be paid immediately. This created serious **risk**, even though the expected **return** from expansion looked attractive on paper. After cutting unnecessary spending and delaying nonessential purchases, the company became more stable. The experience taught the owner that wise financial management depends not only on profit, but also on timing, discipline, and a clear understanding of risk and return.

When a middle-aged **patient** arrived at a community **clinic**, the **nurse** first recorded the main **symptom**: severe chest pain after exercise. The **physician** immediately began the **diagnosis** process and used special equipment to **monitor** the patient's heartbeat. For a short time, the medical team worried that an **emergency** **surgery** might be necessary. Fortunately, further examination showed that the condition could be controlled with proper **treatment**, rest, and a careful **prescription**. The doctor also explained the importance of long-term **prevention**, noting that stress, poor diet, and lack of exercise often lead to more serious illness. Because the patient was concerned about cost, the clinic helped review available **insurance** coverage before arranging follow-up **therapy**. Over the next month, steady **recovery** depended not only on medicine but also on regular checkups and family support. The case reminds us that good health care is not simply about reacting to danger. It also means prevention, communication, and responsible care before and after illness appears.

On the **assembly** line, advanced **machinery** can raise **output**, but regular **maintenance** is still essential. If one small part fails to meet the required **standard**, a serious **defect** may spread through the whole batch. That is why **quality** control remains at the center of manufacturing. When the system works well, finished goods move quickly to **shipment**, reducing delay and improving overall **efficiency**. Yet efficiency should never mean carelessness. The best manufacturers understand that customers remember reliability longer than they remember low prices. For them, long-term success comes from stable processes, careful inspection, and a willingness to solve problems before they become costly mistakes.

Some companies rely on celebrity **influence**, while others focus on customer stories to improve online **engagement**. Smart teams also collect **feedback** after each launch and often design a **survey** to learn whether the message was clear, persuasive, and memorable. ### 文章 7：The Hidden Work Behind Online Orders

For an online store, every **order** begins a long chain of decisions. After a **customer** clicks "buy," the item moves to a **warehouse**, where staff check the **package**, confirm the **schedule**, and prepare it for pickup. A **courier** then chooses the fastest **route** so the **delivery** can arrive on time. During the journey, digital **tracking** allows both buyer and seller to see where the goods are. If demand rises suddenly, the company must improve **fulfillment**, speed up **dispatch**, and make **distribution** more efficient. Working with a large **retailer** may increase sales, but it also creates pressure on storage and transport. When a **shipment** is late or damaged, the customer may ask for a **return**, which raises cost and harms trust. That is why logistics is not simply about moving goods from one place to another. It is a complex system that depends on timing, information, and careful coordination across every step of service.

Many countries now view **renewable** energy as both an economic opportunity and an environmental necessity. Governments also try to **regulate** heavy industry so that fewer natural **resource**s are wasted and less **pollution** enters the air and water. At the same time, companies are increasing **investment** in **sustainable** design, hoping to improve energy **efficiency** and support long-term **conservation**. Progress is rarely cheap or simple, yet the direction is clear. A society that ignores emissions today may face higher health costs and greater environmental damage tomorrow. For that reason, clean energy is not only a technical issue but also a practical choice about how we want to live in the future.

### 文章 9：What Makes Travel Memorable

In the travel industry, a smooth journey begins long before a **tourist** reaches the **destination**. A reliable **reservation** system helps guests confirm an **itinerary**, compare **budget** options, and choose suitable **accommodation**. After arrival, the hotel **reception** desk becomes the center of **hospitality**, offering information about local **transport**, nearby restaurants, and quick **service** when problems arise. Many travelers hope to enjoy regional **cuisine**, visit a famous **attraction**, and buy a small **souvenir** before leaving. During the **peak season**, however, even a beautiful city can feel crowded and confusing. That is why an experienced **guide** is valuable. A good guide saves time, explains local culture, and helps visitors avoid unnecessary trouble. Tourism is often judged by photos and advertisements, but real success depends on details such as timely service, clear planning, and genuine care for guests. When these elements come together, travel becomes not just movement, but a memorable human experience.

### 文章 10：From Soil to Table

Modern farming depends on science as much as tradition. If the **soil** loses nutrients, farmers may add **fertilizer**, improve **irrigation**, and choose a **crop** that can survive local weather. In a dry year, **drought** can reduce **yield** and threaten the entire **supply chain** from farm to city. Some producers still use **pesticide** to protect plants, while others prefer **organic** methods in response to changing consumer demand. On mixed farms, **livestock** provides additional income, but it also requires clean water, good feed, and careful health control. After the **harvest**, proper **storage** becomes essential because poor conditions may damage quality before food reaches a **processor** or a **wholesale** market. In the long run, agriculture is not only about producing more food. It is also about public **nutrition**, environmental balance, and the responsibility to protect land for future generations.
